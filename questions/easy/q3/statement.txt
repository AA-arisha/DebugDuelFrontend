During a routine stabilization cycle, Paradox-Verse Kiro runs a calibration process issued by the Incognito Board. The goal of this process is to measure how instability changes across adjacent timelines.
Each timeline reports a stability value. Instead of analyzing individual values, the calibration system evaluates how rapidly stability shifts from one timeline to the next by applying a predefined mathematical formula.
Given the stability values of N timelines in order, the system computes a calibration score by evaluating the contribution of each timeline based on its immediate neighbors.

For each index i, the contribution is defined as:

f(i) = (a[i] − a[i−1]) / (a[i+1] − a[i])
Where / means floor(or integer) division.

In other words, if an index has both a left and a right neighbor:
    The contribution is the difference between the current value and its left neighbor, divided by the difference between its right neighbor and the current value.
    If either of the difference is 0, the contribution is 0.

If an index does not have neighbors on both sides (i.e., it is the first or last element), its contribution is 0.
The final calibration score is the sum of all such contributions.
